Archaeological research in Skopje and its surroundings confirms that this area has been inhabited since ancient times. The finds take us back 7,000 years, to the beginning of the Neolithic, or first urban, revolution, and so to the foundation of civilisation. So far 15 settlements and sites from this period have been identified. The most important are:

- Tumba, near the village of Madžari

- 5,200 BC

- Cerje, near the village of Govrlevo - 5,000 BC

- Slatina, near Zelenikovo - Middle Neolithic

With the beginning of antiquity (end of the 6th and beginning of the 5th century BC) the first small towns appear: planned, fortified settlements. The changes in Macedonia in the 4th century BC, under Philip II and Alexander III of Macedon, sped up the growth of the small towns. These ancient settlements had a good location, spatial organisation, roads and links, and lively cultural and economic development.

13-11 BC

- The birth of Skopje dates to these years, under the Roman emperor Octavian Augustus. Two legions were moved from the Roman province of Macedonia to the Skopje region, Dardanian rule was ended and Roman rule established. The name of the city Scupi (the ancient name of Skopje) is mentioned for the first time.

84 (or 85) AD

- The Roman emperor Domitian founds the colony Flavia Scupi. The city had Roman self-governing rights and was one of the most important on the Balkan Peninsula: the first true city in this region, a strategic, economic, cultural and religious centre for the next four centuries.

313

- Under the Edict of Milan, Scupi became the seat of a bishopric.

325

- The Skopje bishop Dacus took part in the First Ecumenical Council in Nicaea.

343

- Bishop Paregorius is mentioned.

379 and 388

- In Scupi ("Scopis" and "Scupis") the emperor Theodosius I signed two decrees making Scupi the capital of Dardania.

518 (28 April)

- A strong earthquake destroys the city of Scupi, which had remarkable buildings, public baths, an amphitheatre and other material and cultural treasures. It was described by Count Marcellinus, according to whom the people were spared, because they had already fled before the Slav invasion.

535

- Justiniana Prima is founded, a new city in this region and the legal and ecclesiastical successor of Scupi. Justinian I built it to honour his birthplace. The city was surrounded by fortresses.

550 - 551

- Slavs from beyond the Danube attack and plunder the Skopje region.

End of the 6th and beginning of the 7th century

- The Slavs finally settle the Skopje region.

969-1018

- The creation and growth of the medieval Macedonian state, which in the last years of the 10th century became an empire, with Samuel proclaimed tsar in Prespa. Samuel's empire then included the whole territory of Macedonia (except Thessaloniki), as well as most of Bulgaria, part of Greece, Duklja, Serbia, Bosnia, part of Albania and part of Dalmatia (up to Zadar)

1004

- The Byzantine emperor Basil II took the city of Skopje through the betrayal of Roman, whom Samuel had appointed governor of the city in 976.

1040 - 1041

- An uprising against Byzantine rule breaks out in Macedonia, led by Peter Delyan, grandson of Tsar Samuel, whom the insurgents proclaimed tsar. Most of the territory of Macedonia was liberated, including the city of Skopje.

1072 - 1073

- The last attempt to free Skopje and this region from Byzantine rule. The uprising is led by Skopje nobles under Đorđi Vojteh. The people threw off Roman (Byzantine) rule and proclaimed the Dioclean magnate Constantine Bodin tsar under the name Peter. Đorđi Vojteh was captured by the Byzantines and died in prison in 1073 after long torture.

1083

- The Norman commander Puntes conquered Skopje, which stayed under Norman rule until 1085.

1093 - 1094

- The Raška župan Vukan devastates and partly burns the area around Skopje.

1164

- The Byzantine prince Alexios Angelos, son of Constantine Angelos and of Theodora, the youngest daughter of the emperor Alexios I Komnenos, built the monastery church of St Panteleimon in the village of Nerezi.

1190

- The Raška župan Stefan Nemanja plundered Skopje and razed it to the ground.

1193

- The Byzantine emperor Alexios III, by a special charter, grants Venetian merchants the privilege of trading in the Byzantine lands, including the Skopje region.

1204-1215

- Skopje becomes part of the principality of the feudal ruler Strez, based at Prosek near Demir Kapija.

1215-1230

- The Epirotes conquered Skopje.

1246

- The Nicaeans took Skopje.

1254

- The Bulgarian tsar Michael Asen took Skopje.

1282

- King Milutin annexed Skopje to the Serbian state.

1346

- Stefan Dušan is crowned emperor in Skopje.

1349

- Dušan's Code is proclaimed in Skopje, based on the principles of Justinian's Code.

1392

- After the Battle of Kosovo the Ottomans take Skopje, then ruled by Vuk Branković.

1438

- Sultan Murad II burned Skopje.

1469

- Jews settle in Skopje and found their colony (the Viaskora brotherhood). Merchants and travellers stop there: Arabs, Greeks, Ragusans and others.

1555

- An earthquake destroyed the city. But the city was soon rebuilt.

1569

- In 1569 Kara Trifun opened a bookshop in Skopje.

1594

- The Dutch engraver Jacobus Harrewijn made the engraving of Skopje and published it in Nuremberg. The same year a great fire hits the city.

1669

- Skopje is visited by the English traveller Dr Brown, who describes the city in detail.

1669-1670

- Small boats, dugouts and rafts carried grain, wood and other goods down the Vardar to Thessaloniki.

1689

- Karpoš's Uprising breaks out. The same year Skopje is burned by the Austrian general Piccolomini.

1706

- 2,000 inhabitants died of the plague (records of Marin Kabot of Dubrovnik).

1767

- The last archbishop, Arsenije, of Macedonian origin, resigns under pressure and intrigue from the Patriarchate of Constantinople, and the Archbishopric of Ohrid is abolished.

1778

- A great flood hits the city.

1785

- The first significant building on the bank of the Vardar is built - the metropolitan's residence.

1835

- The Church of the Holy Mother of God is consecrated. The iconostasis was finished in 1842, and the throne icons were made in 1867. The municipal school, the first public Slavonic school, is opened.

1839

- Description by the botanist Grisebach.

1849

- The first telegraph in Skopje starts working.

1858

- The city is flooded again.

1863

- A Macedonian Slavonic school is opened, run by the Macedonian revivalist Jordan Hadži Konstantinov - Džinot.

1872

- The Skopje eparchy is separated from the Patriarchate of Constantinople.

1873

- The first railway line, Thessaloniki - Skopje, is opened. The first train arrives in Skopje from Thessaloniki on 9 August, at the newly built railway station.

1880

- The first Catholic religious primary school is founded; it worked until 1926.

1882

- The factory for Turkish delight and sweets is founded (today's "Evropa" factory)

1890

- In 1890 Teodosij Gologanov arrived in Skopje and consecrated the foundations of the Church of St Demetrius. The vali of Skopje expelled him under pressure and bribery from the Greek and Serbian consuls. He returned to Skopje, welcomed by the people, and tried to separate from the Exarchate and proclaim an autocephalous Macedonian church. The next year he was replaced, and in 1892 he was expelled again.

1888-1891

- Gjorče Petrov is a teacher in Skopje.

1893

- The Secret Macedonian-Adrianople Revolutionary Organisation (TMORO) is founded in Thessaloniki; it soon becomes VMRO (Internal Macedonian Revolutionary Organisation).

1903

- In Thessaloniki, VMRO decides to raise an uprising in Macedonia. The same year, in Smilevo, it is decided to start the uprising on 2 August. On 4 May that year Goce Delčev is killed, and on 2 August the Ilinden Uprising breaks out and the Kruševo Republic is proclaimed, with the famous Kruševo Manifesto. The Skopje voivodes Vasil Adžilarski and Dame Martinov take part in the Ilinden Uprising and the Kruševo Republic with their bands.

1906

- The first theatre building is built, and the building of the Skopje metropolitanate is renovated.

1907 - 1908

- The first tram in Skopje, bought in Vienna for 50 Turkish gold lira. It was pulled by 2 horses on cart wheels and ran from the Burmali Mosque to the "Islahane" park until 1910 or 1911. The second tram belonged to the Municipality and was in use from 1909 to 1912; it ran on rails and was pulled by one horse.

1908

- In the great fire that struck the city, many houses, about 700 shops, one church and three mosques burned down.

1909

- The sports clubs "Silni" and "Vardarski junak" are founded.

- On 7 January the Social Democratic organisation is formed.

- The first official football match is played at "Idadija", in front of the entrance to the City Park, between two teams of Turkish soldiers (cadets).

- The first power plant, with a capacity of 60 kWh, is started.

- The first film screening is held in the "Angleterre" café.

1910

- On 27 August Mother Teresa (Gonxha Bojaxhiu), the famous missionary and Nobel Peace Prize laureate, is born in Skopje.

- The first newspaper of the Social Democratic organisation, "Socijalistička zora", is published.

- At the conference in Thessaloniki on uniting the socialists of Macedonia, the Skopje organisation is represented by three delegates:

M. Cokov, S. Divlev, D. Cekić.

1911

- The first car, a "Renault", starts driving through the streets of Skopje.

- This year the Turkish sultan Reşad V stays in Skopje

1912

- On 24 and 25 October, after the First Balkan War, the Turkish troops leave Skopje, and a day later the city is taken by the Morava Division of the Serbian army.

1913-1915

- Branislav Nušić, the famous comedy writer, is director of the Skopje National Theatre.

1914

- Skopje gets its first urban plan through an international competition.

- The report "The entry of King Peter I into Skopje" is filmed; it is also the first archival film footage showing parts of the city.

1916

- The city is flooded again.

1920

- The Faculty of Philosophy is opened.

1923

- The first modern hotel-restaurant, "Bristol", is built.

1924

- A steam brewery with an annual capacity of 36,000 hectolitres of beer is built.

1925

- The Burmali Mosque is demolished and the construction of the Officers' Club begins in its place.

1926

- The Zoo is founded, as well as the Zoological Museum.

1927

- On 26 October the National Theatre is ceremonially opened.

1928

- The premiere of "Begalka" is staged in Macedonian (Kumanovo dialect), by the writer Vasil Iljoski, under the title "Lenče Kumanovče".

1933

- A diesel power plant of 1500 kWh is built.

1937

- Construction of the Skopje railway station begins. It opened on 1 December 1940. After the earthquake of 26 July 1963 only part of the station building remains as a memorial; it was adapted to house the Museum of the City of Skopje.

- The city is flooded again.

1938

- The "Matka" hydroelectric plant (5,600 kWh) is put into operation.

1939

- To mark the 150th anniversary of the French Revolution (14 July), the Kruševo Manifesto of 1903 is published in Skopje.

- The main post office is built.

- The first concrete road, 1.8 km long, is built (from today's hotel "Continental" to the "Alkaloid" factory).

1940-1941

- A period of mass strikes and demonstrations, peaking on 25, 26 and 27 March 1941 with the protests against the signing of the Tripartite Pact (Germany, Italy, Japan). More than 20,000 people of Skopje take part.

- On 2 January Radio Skopje starts broadcasting news.

1941

- Without a declaration of war, on 6 April Skopje is bombed by the Germans (about 600 inhabitants killed and over 1,000 wounded, and public and residential buildings destroyed).

- On 11 October the Macedonian people rise up against the fascist occupiers.

1944

- On 7 April the cathedral church of the Holy Mother of God (the city's patron) burns down. The iconostasis burns too, and many valuable objects (icons and others) are taken away by the Bulgarian occupiers.

- On 29 October the first issue of the newspaper "Nova Makedonija" is published (printed in the village of Gorno Vranovci).

- On 13 November Skopje is liberated and becomes the capital of the People's Republic of Macedonia.

1945

- On 27 January at 7:00 Radio Skopje begins broadcasting a regular programme in the Macedonian language.

- In the same month the first issue of the children's newspaper "Pioneer" is published.

- On 5 March the Physical Culture Union of Macedonia (FSM) is founded.

- On 3 April the Macedonian National Theatre opens with the play "Platon Krechet" by A. Korneychuk, translated by Blaže Koneski.

- The welcome in Skopje of the units of the 48th Division of the Macedonian army is filmed, after the breakthrough on the Srem front and the liberation of the country.

- On 16 April the first government of Macedonia is appointed, and Lazar Koliševski is elected its first president.

- On 3 May the first alphabet of the Macedonian language is published.

- On 30 July the first Orthography of the Macedonian language is printed.

- On 1, 2 and 3 August the First Macedonian Physical Culture Rally is held.

1946

- On 24 January the first Grammar of the literary Macedonian language, by Krum Kepeski, is published.

- On 2 and 3 August the First Congress of the People's Front of Macedonia is held in Skopje.

- On 10 October the relics of Goce Delčev are brought from Sofia to Skopje and laid in the sarcophagus in the courtyard of the Church of the Holy Saviour.

- On 31 December the first Constitution of Macedonia is adopted.

1947

- The First Five-Year Plan for the industrialisation and electrification of Macedonia is adopted.

- On 21 August the film production company "Vardar Film" is founded; in 1951-52 the first Macedonian feature film, "Frosina", is made.

1948

- The first urban plan of the city after the liberation is drawn up.

- On 1 July the Institute of National History of the Macedonian People is founded.

- The City Transport Company (GSP) is founded.

1949

- On 24 April the "Cyril and Methodius" University of Skopje is founded.

- On 1 May the first ballet performance, "Walpurgis Night", is staged with pupils of the "Karpoš" Pioneer House.

- The Museum of the City of Skopje is founded.

1950

- The folk dance ensemble "Tanec" is founded.

- The first trade fair of industry and mining (Skopje Fair) is held.

1953

- The "Krste Misirkov" Institute of the Macedonian Language is founded.

1956

- Horse-drawn cabs are banned as a means of transport in traffic.

1958

- On 4 October the Archbishopric of Ohrid is restored, and Dositej is elected its first head.

1961

- On 13 May Radio Television Skopje broadcasts its first TV programme.

1962

- On 16 November Skopje was hit by a great flood, which caused considerable material damage.

1963

- On 26 July a catastrophic earthquake of magnitude 9 on the Mercalli scale destroys the city: 1,070 dead and over 3,500 injured. Over the next two decades 921 earthquakes were recorded in the Skopje epicentral area, 3 of them of intensity 7 on the Mercalli scale.

1964

- On 12 February the Museum of Contemporary Art is founded, based on the collection of works donated from all over the world as a contribution to overcoming the consequences of the catastrophic earthquake.

1967

- On 28 February the ring road on Mount Vodno is opened.

- On 18 July the autocephaly of the Macedonian Orthodox Church is proclaimed, and Mr Dositej, Archbishop of Ohrid and Macedonia, is elected its head.

- On 10 October the Macedonian Academy of Sciences and Arts (MANU) is founded. Blaže Koneski is elected its first president.

- On 20 October the Skopje Mines and Ironworks combine starts trial operation.

1970

- On 31 August the UN Secretary-General U Thant visits Skopje and is made an honorary citizen of the city of Skopje.

- On 29 November Radio Skopje's 1000 kW medium-wave transmitter (then the strongest in the Balkans) starts broadcasting.

1971

- On 28 November the "Goce Delčev" bridge is opened to traffic.

1972

- From 18 September to 13 October Skopje hosts the 20th men's and 5th women's Chess Olympiad and the FIDE congress.

- In the autumn, mass afforestation of the bare hills around Skopje begins.

- On 19 November Valentina Tereshkova, the first woman in space, stays in Skopje.

1975

- On 22-29 June the 9th World Kayak and Canoe Championship (downhill) and the 14th World Wildwater Kayak and Canoe Championship (slalom) are held on the Treska river.

- Lidija Velkovska is elected Miss Macedonia and Miss Yugoslavia. She takes part in Miss World in London and is chosen fourth runner-up.

1976

- The Cinematheque of SR Macedonia is founded.

1978

- On 26 July the "Treska" sports and recreation lake opens, built with money contributed by the citizens of Skopje. The same contributions built several schools, sports grounds, swimming pools and many other facilities in the city and villages.

1981

- On 27 July (at 12:22) the first train arrives at the new railway station. Before that, at 10:00, the last train left the Old Railway Station.

- From 11 to 15 September the World Freestyle Wrestling Championship is held.

1982

- On 13 November the refinery in the village of Miladinovci starts operating.

1984

- On 26 July the "Rašče 2" water intake starts operating. Skopje gets quality drinking water, about 3.5 m3 per second.

- On 9 November three new automatic telephone exchanges are put into service. On this day Skopje has 92,800 telephone lines.

1985

- On 26 January an expedition of the Skopje mountaineering society "25 Maj" climbs Aconcagua (7,035 m), the highest peak in South America (in the Andes).

1986

- The Skopje fire brigade helps put out the great oil fire in the port of Thessaloniki, for which Greece gives it high recognition.

- In June the city is reached by a radioactive cloud, a consequence of the nuclear disaster of 24 April 1986 at Chernobyl (former USSR). The health authorities take preventive measures (fruit and vegetables must be washed, etc.).

1987

- On 15 April the electrified Skopje-Gevgelija railway is put into service.

1989

- On 6 May Radmila Dobreska from Skopje and Zoran Miletić from Niš climb the highest peak of the Caucasus - East Elbrus (5,621 m).

- A Macedonian mountaineering expedition climbs the highest peak of the Himalayas - Mount Everest. The first to reach the summit is the Macedonian climber Dimitar Ilievski, who died on the way back to base camp.

- Amendment 56 defines the sovereignty of the Macedonian state.

1990

- On 12 August the Cathedral Church of "St Clement of Ohrid" is solemnly consecrated in the presence of many senior clergy headed by Archbishop Gavril. About 80,000 people attended: citizens of Skopje, people from other Macedonian towns and Macedonians from the diaspora (Australia, Canada, the USA and several European countries).

- In the autumn, multi-party parliamentary elections are held for the first time since independence. 16 political parties take part with candidates for the municipal assemblies of the Republic of Macedonia, the city of Skopje and its five municipalities.

1991

- Kiro Gligorov is elected the first President of the Republic of Macedonia.

- On 1 April the first Macedonian airline, "Palair Macedonia", makes its first scheduled flight Skopje-Belgrade-Skopje, with an "Antonov-24" passenger plane seating 24.

- On 16 April a central ceremony for the Bayram holiday is held in the Sultan Murad Mosque (the largest Islamic shrine in the Balkans).

- On 26 April RTV Skopje is renamed MRT (Macedonian Radio-Television).

- On 7 June SR Macedonia is renamed the Republic of Macedonia.

- June - July. SFR Yugoslavia breaks up (war in Slovenia, then in Croatia and in Bosnia and Herzegovina).

- On 8 and 9 September a referendum is held on proclaiming the Republic of Macedonia a sovereign and independent state (95% voted "YES").

- On 17 November the Constitution of the Republic of Macedonia is proclaimed.

1993

- On 8 April the Republic of Macedonia is admitted to the UN under the name Former Yugoslav Republic of Macedonia.

- On 8 May the first official Macedonian currency, the "denar", is adopted.

- June-July: a US military unit (over 300 soldiers and officers) arrives in Skopje as a peacekeeping force within UNPROFOR (UNPREDEP).

- 18 August is proclaimed the Day of the Army of the Republic of Macedonia.

1994

- On 30 October a rally on the city square calls for a boycott of the elections, with members of VMRO-DPMNE and the Democratic Party.

- The "SOROS" Foundation - Open Society Institute for Macedonia is founded.

- Reconstruction begins of the Stone Bridge, the "Ristić Palace" and other buildings in the city centre.

1995

- The Assembly of the City of Skopje adopts a Programme for the preservation of Skopje's architectural heritage.

- On 3 October at 09:45 on "Maršal Tito" Street in front of the hotel "Bristol" a car-bomb assassination attempt is made on Kiro Gligorov, President of the Republic of Macedonia. The President is seriously injured and his driver is killed. Several citizens are injured by the powerful explosion, and one passer-by later dies of his injuries.

- On 11 October the Military Academy of the Army of the Republic of Macedonia (ARM) starts regular classes.

- On 31 December a festive welcome of the new year 1996 is organised on the city square.

1996

- On 8 September mobile telephony with 25,000 lines starts at the Telecommunications Centre in Skopje.

- On 17 November the first multi-party local elections are held in the city of Skopje. In the first round neither the mayor of the city nor the mayors of its seven municipalities were elected; only the city and municipal councils were. In the second round (1 December 1996) there were four candidates for mayor of Skopje: Risto Penov, Ljupčo Nikolovski-Fufo, Jovica Ugrinovski and Darko Marković. Risto Penov, of the coalition of DP, VMRO-DPMNE and MAAK-Conservative, was elected the first mayor in the city's history.

1997

- During the year there is large-scale reconstruction and building: "Vodnjanska" Street, "Partizanski odredi" Boulevard (from its start to the Cathedral Church of St Clement of Ohrid), the streets "Ruzveltova" and "Železnička", the main street in Šuto Orizari municipality, the street by the "Butel" city cemetery and others. The Skopje fortress "Kale" is reconstructed, and the quay from the "Goce Delčev" bridge to "Leninova" Street gets new quality paving and new street lamps.

- On 13 November the four-lane "Vodnjanska" boulevard is opened to traffic.

1998

- VMRO-DPMNE comes to power.

1999

- On 25 March NATO attacks FR Yugoslavia.

- Hundreds of thousands of refugees flee to Macedonia, and refugee camps are built around Skopje to house them.

- On 5 September the monument to Mother Teresa (Gonxha Bojaxhiu), by the sculptor Tome Serafimovski, is unveiled.
